# Product

## Register

product

## Users

SealAI is used by internal platform operators and builders who need to create projects, deploy application and database workloads, inspect runtime state, and act on resources without dropping into disconnected tooling. They are usually in a focused operations workflow: choosing a deployment path, reading project state, changing AP or DB settings, checking logs and metrics, opening terminals, or using the assistant to trigger project work.

## Product Purpose

SealAI combines a project canvas, deployment panes, resource settings, logs, metrics, terminal sessions, and assistant-driven actions into one product surface for operating application platforms. Success means users can understand what exists, see what is changing, and take the next resource action with confidence from the current screen.

## Brand Personality

Technical, atmospheric, and spatial. The product should feel like a deep operational cockpit with controlled ambient light, layered depth, and a dark interface that supports long focused sessions. Blue breathing gradients, environmental glow, and restrained translucency may carry the mood when they clarify hierarchy or state.

## Anti-references

Avoid light SaaS dashboards, generic shadcn gray-white card stacks, decorative marketing-page layouts, low-quality glassmorphism, overbright cyber-neon styling, excessive ornamental animation, and repeated template grids of icon plus heading plus body copy.

## Design Principles

1. Task clarity before atmosphere: depth, glow, and translucency must support wayfinding, resource state, or focus.
2. Make the platform feel spatial: panes, canvas, terminals, and assistant surfaces should read as layers in one coherent workspace.
3. Keep technical confidence: labels, states, and resource actions should be precise, domain-correct, and calm under failure.
4. Use motion as operational feedback: animation should explain transitions, loading, selection, and state change.
5. Preserve product density: repeated workflows should stay compact, scannable, and efficient.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Start with a minimum accessibility baseline: readable contrast for primary text and controls, visible focus treatment where components already support it, and clear disabled and error states. Advanced accessibility work such as reduced-motion alternatives, keyboard-first workflows, color-blind-specific state design, and multilingual support is deferred until a later accessibility pass.
